Family reunification (reagrupación familiar) lets a non-EU citizen who lives legally in Spain bring their spouse or partner, children and, in some cases, parents. Spain's Ministry of Inclusion states that documents from other countries must be translated into Spanish by a sworn translator and apostilled or legalized.

I'm Elisa Vílchez, sworn translator for English appointed by Spain's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MAEC), no. 6774. I translate the civil records, criminal record checks and medical certificates for family reunification files from the US and the UK.

This guide covers the general regime: the family member who lives in Spain is not Spanish or an EU citizen. Family members of Spanish or EU citizens follow a different procedure.

How it works

  1. In Spain: the family member who lives there (the sponsor) applies for the reunification permit at the immigration office (Oficina de Extranjería) of their province. They usually need to have lived in Spain for at least a year and have renewed their permit for at least another year.
  2. The permit is granted: the immigration office has two months to decide.
  3. At the consulate: the relative has two months from the notification to apply in person for the visa at the Spanish consulate where they live.
  4. Travel: once the visa is issued, they collect it within a month, enter Spain within a month and apply for their residence card within a month of arrival.

Mistakes that delay applications

  • Missing the two-month window. Order the criminal record check and the civil records early, while the permit is being processed, so the visa application is ready when the permit is notified.
  • A criminal record check that is too old by the time of the visa appointment. FBI checks and apostilles take weeks; time them with the permit decision.
  • Documents without an apostille. Spain only accepts foreign public documents that are apostilled (or legalized) by the issuing country.
  • Non-sworn translations. The Ministry asks for translations by a sworn translator. A translation with only a translator's statement of accuracy may be rejected.
  • Translating in batches. Many families translate the civil records for the permit and then the criminal record and medical certificate for the visa. Planning both together saves a second round.

Frequently asked questions

Do the documents for the permit and the visa need to be translated twice?

No. A sworn translation of a document is valid for both steps. Keep the original translation (or the electronically signed file) for the consulate.

Is your translation accepted by the immigration office and the consulate?

Yes. Translations by MAEC-appointed sworn translators are official translations in Spain and at Spanish consulates.

Digital or paper?

Both are possible. The immigration office's online platform (Mercurio) works with digital documents, so a translation with a qualified electronic signature is practical for the permit. Check what your consulate accepts for the visa. See the general FAQ on digital signature.
