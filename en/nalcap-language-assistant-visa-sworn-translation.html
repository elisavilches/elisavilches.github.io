If you're going to Spain as a NALCAP language assistant (auxiliar de conversación) for more than 180 days, your visa application usually needs two translated documents: your FBI background check, with its federal apostille, and your medical certificate.

What you'll need translated

FBI background checkFor stays of more than 180 days. No older than 6 months, with the federal apostille from the U.S. Department of State (a state apostille isn't valid), translated into Spanish. If you lived in other countries in the last 5 years, you need their certificates too.
Medical certificateA doctor's statement that you don't have any disease that could have serious public health repercussions, with an official translation into Spanish.
Your placement letter (nombramiento)It's already in Spanish, so it doesn't need a translation.

This is what consulates usually ask for. Each one may ask for slightly different things, so check yours.

Timeline tips

  • Start with the FBI check. With the federal apostille it's the slowest document, and it must be under 6 months old when you apply.
  • The medical certificate can be translated as soon as you have it. Send me both together if you can and I'll give you one quote.
  • Check your consulate's appointment date and work backwards from it.

Frequently asked questions

My consulate says "certified translator". Is a sworn translation valid?

Yes. A sworn translation is the official translation under Spanish law, so it meets the requirement at every consulate. It's also valid if you need the documents later in Spain.

Can I send the documents by email?

Yes. A clear scan is enough. I can deliver the translation with a qualified electronic signature or on paper.

I'm staying less than 180 days. Do I need the FBI check?

Usually not: the criminal record check is normally required only for stays of more than 180 days. Check your consulate's NALCAP page to be sure.
