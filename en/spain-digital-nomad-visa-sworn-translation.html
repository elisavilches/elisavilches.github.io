For Spain's digital nomad visa, the documents that usually need an apostille and a translation into Spanish are your criminal record check, your company's registry certificate, your degree or proof of experience, and certificates for family members. Your employer letter must be in Spanish or translated.

I'm Elisa Vílchez, MAEC sworn translator no. 6774. Under Spanish law, sworn translations are official translations, so every consulate accepts them.

Two ways to apply

  • From your country, at the Spanish consulate: a telework visa valid for up to one year.
  • From Spain, if you're there legally: a residence authorisation from the Large Companies and Strategic Groups Unit (UGE), valid for up to three years.

What follows is what consulates usually ask for. If you apply from Spain, check the UGE's current requirements.

Translation rules

  • The apostille: some consulates don't ask for it to be translated, others do. Some also want documents apostilled first and translated afterwards. Translating the apostille with the document avoids a second round.
  • Who can translate: some consulates accept certified translators; others ask for a sworn translator, at least for the criminal record check. A sworn translation is valid at all of them.
  • Electronic signature: the MAEC allows sworn translations to be signed electronically. I can deliver them that way or on paper.

Income requirement

200% of Spain's minimum wage (SMI). With the 2026 SMI that is about €2,442 a month, plus €916 for the first family member and €306 for each additional one. Check the current figure with your consulate.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a translation done in the US?

Some consulates accept certified translations. If you plan to apply from Spain or use the documents later at Spanish offices, a sworn translation is the safe choice, because it's the official one under Spanish law.

Does my contract need a sworn translation?

Not always: some consulates accept the employer letter in Spanish or with a non-sworn translation. Check your consulate's page, and ask me if you're unsure.
