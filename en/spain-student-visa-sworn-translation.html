If you're going to Spain to study, take a language course or work as a language assistant, you'll usually apply for a student visa. For stays of more than 180 days, the documents that most often need an apostille and a translation into Spanish are your criminal record check and your medical certificate.

I'm Elisa Vílchez, sworn translator for English appointed by Spain's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MAEC), no. 6774. I translate student visa documents for university students, study abroad participants and language assistants, who have recommended me to each other for years.

Who this guide is for

  • University students: a semester or a year abroad, a full degree, a master's or a doctorate.
  • Language and study programs: study abroad programs and language schools.
  • Language assistants: NALCAP (auxiliares de conversación) and other language assistant programs. See the section below.

Documents that usually need a translation

DocumentWhat to expect
Criminal record checkUsually for stays of more than 180 days, and usually no older than 6 months. US: the FBI check with a federal apostille. UK: the ACRO certificate. If you lived in other countries in the last 5 years, you may need their certificates too.
Medical certificateA doctor's statement that you don't have any disease with serious public health repercussions. Usually recent, and with an official translation unless your consulate provides a bilingual form.
Proof of fundsBank statements, a scholarship letter or a sponsor's letter. Some consulates accept them in English, others ask for a translation. A scholarship or a paid program may cover this requirement.
Diplomas and transcriptsSometimes, for a master's or a doctorate. See the diplomas and transcripts guide.
MinorsParental authorization and a birth certificate, usually apostilled and translated. See the birth certificate guide.

Your admission or placement letter from the Spanish school, university or program is usually already in Spanish and needs no translation. Health insurance certificates are usually issued in Spanish by the insurer.

This is what consulates usually ask for. Each one sets its own list, so check yours.

Timeline tips

  • Start with the criminal record check. With its apostille it's the slowest document, and it must still be recent when you apply.
  • Work backwards from your consulate appointment. Leave time for the apostille and the translation.
  • Send everything together. The criminal record check, the medical certificate and any other document: one quote and one delivery.

Language assistants (NALCAP and other programs)

Language assistants (auxiliares de conversación) usually apply for a student visa with their placement letter (nombramiento). For a full school year, that typically means two translated documents: the criminal record check, with its apostille, and the medical certificate. Your placement letter is already in Spanish.

Start the FBI check as soon as you accept your placement: with the federal apostille it can take weeks, and the visa appointment comes soon after the placement letter.

Frequently asked questions

My consulate says "certified translator". Is a sworn translation valid?

Yes. A sworn translation is the official translation under Spanish law, so it meets the requirement at every consulate. It's also valid if you need the documents later in Spain, for example for your student residence card (TIE) or a renewal.

I'm staying less than 180 days. Do I need the criminal record check?

Usually not: it is normally required only for stays of more than 180 days. Check your consulate's list to be sure.

Can I send the documents by email?

Yes. A clear scan is enough. I can deliver the translation with a qualified electronic signature or on paper. See the FAQ on digital signature.
