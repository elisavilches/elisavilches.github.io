Spanish authorities ask for your diploma and your academic transcript in several procedures: getting a foreign degree recognized (homologación or equivalencia), some visas, and jobs or exams that require a degree. In most cases they want the documents apostilled and with an official translation into Spanish.

I'm Elisa Vílchez, sworn translator for English appointed by Spain's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MAEC), no. 6774. I translate diplomas, transcripts and their apostilles from the US, the UK and other English-speaking countries.

When you may need it

  • Degree recognition: homologación (your degree is recognized as a specific Spanish degree, needed for regulated professions such as medicine, nursing, law or teaching) or equivalencia (recognition of the academic level and field).
  • Visas: for example, the digital nomad visa accepts a university degree as proof of qualification.
  • Studies in Spain: some universities ask for a translated transcript when you apply for a master's or a doctorate. Others accept documents in English.
  • Work: employers, professional associations (colegios profesionales) and public exams that require a degree.

Key facts

DocumentsThe diploma (degree certificate) and the transcript with every course, its credits or hours, and the grades.
ApostilleUsually required on both. It certifies the document (or the notary's or official's signature on it), not its content.
TranslationSworn translation into Spanish of each document, usually including the apostille.
DeliveryQualified electronic signature or paper with original stamp and signature. Many recognition procedures are filed online, where a digitally signed translation is practical.

Step by step

  1. Ask what exactly is required. Check the list of the body you are applying to: which documents, whether they want the original or a certified copy, and whether the apostille must be translated too.
  2. Get the documents apostilled. Diplomas and transcripts from private institutions are often not public documents, so they are usually notarized first and then apostilled. In the US the apostille comes from the Secretary of State of the state where the document was notarized or issued; in the UK, from the FCDO.
  3. Send me a clear scan of every page of each document, apostille included, front and back. I'll reply with a quote and a delivery date.
  4. I translate the documents and the apostilles, then sign and stamp the sworn translation.
  5. Submit each document with its apostille and its sworn translation.

Save time: send the diploma and the transcript together, plus any other document you will need for the same procedure. One quote and one delivery covers the whole set.

Be prepared: things that sometimes come up

Requirements vary between bodies and change over time, so it's better to go over-prepared:

  • An incomplete transcript. For recognition, the transcript usually has to show every course with its credits or hours and the grades. A summary or an unofficial printout may be rejected.
  • Course descriptions. Some procedures also ask for the syllabus or course descriptions. They can be long: ask whether they need a translation before ordering one.
  • The apostille on the wrong thing. Some bodies want the apostille on the original diploma, others accept a notarized copy. If you're not sure, ask before you start.
  • Translating the apostille. Some authorities ask for it, others don't. Translating it at the same time avoids a second round.
  • Names that don't match. If your name on the diploma differs from your passport (a maiden name, a middle name, an accent), tell me. I translate exactly what the document says, and you may need to explain the difference.
  • Grades. A sworn translation keeps your grades as they are, with the grading scale if the document shows it. Converting your grades to the Spanish scale is a separate procedure.
  • Time. Recognition can take months. Start early if a job, an exam or a visa depends on it.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to translate the diploma and the transcript?

For degree recognition, usually both. For a visa, often only the diploma. Check the list for your procedure, and send me both if in doubt: I'll tell you what each one involves.

Is your translation accepted for degree recognition?

Yes. Translations by MAEC-appointed sworn translators are official translations in Spain.

Digital or paper? How long does it take?

Both formats are possible. A diploma is a short document; a transcript can run to several pages. I'll confirm the delivery date with your quote. See the general FAQ on digital signature and delivery times.
