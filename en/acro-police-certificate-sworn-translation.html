If you live in the UK, Spanish consulates ask for your ACRO Police Certificate with a Hague apostille from the FCDO and a sworn translation into Spanish. Get the apostille first and the translation after it.

I'm Elisa Vílchez, sworn translator for English appointed by Spain's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MAEC), no. 6774. I translate ACRO certificates for non-lucrative, digital nomad, student and family reunification visas.

Key facts

DocumentACRO Police Certificate. It also covers Scotland and Northern Ireland. Some consulates do not accept a Disclosure Scotland certificate instead.
ApostillePaper apostille from the FCDO Legalisation Office, on the original ACRO certificate. ACRO certificates can't get an e-Apostille.
How recentUsually no older than 6 months; some checklists have asked for 3. Check the current checklist for your visa.
TranslationInto Spanish by a sworn translator. Some consulates also ask for the apostille to be translated; if yours does, or you prefer to be on the safe side, I translate it too.
DeliveryQualified electronic signature or paper with original stamp and signature
PriceFixed quote per document or per visa package, on request

Step by step

  1. Apply for the ACRO Police Certificate online. ACRO says it can take up to 20 working days and sends it by post only.
  2. Get the apostille from the FCDO Legalisation Office. GOV.UK says it usually takes up to 25 working days. ACRO can post the certificate straight to the FCDO if you give your FCDO reference as the delivery address.
  3. Send me a clear scan of the apostilled certificate. I'll send you a quote and a delivery date.
  4. I translate the certificate (and the apostille if your consulate needs it), then sign and stamp the sworn translation.
  5. Submit the original certificate with its apostille and my sworn translation to BLS or the consulate.

Plan ahead: the certificate and the apostille together can take around two months. They need to be done early enough, but the certificate must still be under 6 months old when you apply.

Mistakes that delay applications

  • Disclosure Scotland instead of ACRO. Some consulates only accept the ACRO certificate for the UK. ACRO is the safe choice.
  • Apostilling a notarised copy. Consulates may ask for the apostille to legalise the signature of whoever issued the certificate, not a notary's or solicitor's signature. Apostille the original.
  • Translating before the apostille. Some checklists ask for documents to be legalised first and translated afterwards. Get the apostille first.
  • A certificate that is too old by the time of your appointment.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to send you the original?

No. A high-resolution scan of every page, including the apostille, is enough. Keep the original for your application.

Can a UK translator do the translation?

Spanish consulates in the UK usually ask for a sworn translator, and some specify a sworn translator-interpreter registered in Spain, which is what I am.

Do I need certificates from other countries?

If you've lived in other countries recently, yes. Consulate checklists differ on how many years they cover, so check yours.
