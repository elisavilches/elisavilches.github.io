To use your FBI background check in a Spanish visa application, you need three things: the FBI Identity History Summary, a federal apostille from the U.S. Department of State, and a sworn translation into Spanish of the check.

I'm Elisa Vílchez, sworn translator for English appointed by Spain's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MAEC), no. 6774. I translate FBI checks for non-lucrative, digital nomad, student and family reunification visas.

Key facts

DocumentFBI Identity History Summary (fingerprint-based criminal record check), for applicants aged 18 or over. Local or state police certificates are not accepted.
ApostilleFederal apostille from the U.S. Department of State. A state (local) apostille is not valid.
How recentIssued within the 6 months before you apply (Spanish consulates in Los Angeles and Miami)
Other countriesIf you lived in another country during the last 5 years, you also need its police certificate, apostilled and translated (Miami: stays of 6 months or more)
TranslationSworn translation of the check into Spanish. The Miami consulate states that the apostille does not need a translation. If your consulate asks for it, I translate it too.
DeliveryQualified electronic signature or paper with original stamp and signature
PriceFixed quote per document or per visa package, on request

Step by step

  1. Request your FBI Identity History Summary from the FBI.
  2. Get the federal apostille from the U.S. Department of State. FBI checks are federal documents, so a state apostille is not valid.
  3. Send me a clear scan of the check. I'll send you a quote and a delivery date.
  4. I translate the check, then sign and stamp the sworn translation.
  5. Submit the original check with its apostille and my sworn translation to the consulate or to BLS.

Save time: if your consulate doesn't require the apostille to be translated (Miami says it doesn't), the translation can be done while the apostille is being processed.

Mistakes that delay applications

  • A state apostille instead of the federal one. FBI checks are federal documents and only the U.S. Department of State can apostille them.
  • A check that is too old. Allow for the apostille processing time so the check is still under 6 months old on your appointment day.
  • A local police certificate instead of the FBI check. Spanish consulates ask for the federal, fingerprint-based FBI check.
  • Low-quality scans. Faint seals and signatures that I can't read can't be included in the translation.

Frequently asked questions

Does the apostille need to be translated too?

It depends on the consulate. Miami says it doesn't. If yours asks for it, I translate the apostille together with the check in the same sworn translation.

Is your translation accepted by Spanish consulates?

Yes. Translations by MAEC-appointed sworn translators are official translations in Spain and at Spanish consulates.

Do I need to send you the original?

No. A high-resolution scan of every page is enough; keep the original for the consulate. More in the general FAQ.

Digital or paper? How long does it take?

Both formats are possible; check what your consulate or BLS office accepts. An FBI check is a short document, and I'll confirm the delivery date with your quote. See the general FAQ on digital signature and delivery times.
