To use your FBI background check in a Spanish visa application, you need three things in this order: the FBI Identity History Summary, a federal apostille from the U.S. Department of State, and a sworn translation into Spanish of both the check and the apostille.

I'm Elisa Vílchez, sworn translator for English appointed by Spain's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MAEC), no. 6774. I translate FBI checks for non-lucrative, digital nomad, student and family reunification visas.

Key facts

DocumentFBI Identity History Summary (criminal record check), for applicants aged 18 or over
ApostilleFederal apostille from the U.S. Department of State. A state (local) apostille is not valid.
How recentIssued within the last 6 months when you apply (Spanish Consulate in Los Angeles)
Other countriesYou also need certificates from every country you lived in during the last 5 years
TranslationInto Spanish, covering the check and the apostille
DeliveryQualified electronic signature or paper with original stamp and signature
PriceFixed quote per document or per visa package, on request

The right order

  1. Request your FBI Identity History Summary from the FBI.
  2. Get the federal apostille from the U.S. Department of State. The apostille certifies the FBI's signature, so it must be the federal one.
  3. Send me a clear scan of the check together with the apostille. I'll send you a quote and a delivery date.
  4. I translate the check and the apostille, then sign and stamp the sworn translation.
  5. Submit the original check with its apostille and my sworn translation to the consulate or to BLS.

Translate after the apostille, not before. Otherwise the apostille is left untranslated and you'll need a second translation.

Mistakes that delay applications

  • A state apostille instead of the federal one. FBI checks are federal documents and only the U.S. Department of State can apostille them.
  • A check that is too old. Allow for the apostille processing time so the check is still under 6 months old on your appointment day.
  • Translating before the apostille. The apostille also has to be translated.
  • Low-quality scans. Faint seals and signatures that I can't read can't be included in the translation.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to send you the original?

No. A high-resolution scan of every page, including the apostille, is enough. Keep the original for the consulate.

Is your translation accepted by Spanish consulates?

Yes. Translations by MAEC-appointed sworn translators are official translations in Spain and at Spanish consulates.

Digital or paper?

Both are possible. Check whether your consulate or BLS office accepts the electronically signed version or needs paper.

How long does it take?

An FBI check is a short document. I'll confirm the exact delivery date with your quote.
